Industries · Product Development Teams

For the team that turns design intent into material

From sketch or sample to an approvable fabric: a sampling workflow with written comparisons, a hand-feel discipline, and a revision history that survives the season.

Request a Fabric MatchBrowse the Fabric Library
01

The problems a development team carries

Intent-to-material gap

The design says “softer, heavier, with more recovery.” The fabric has to say the same thing in GSM, fibre and construction — and prove it.

Hand-feel transfer

The hardest specification to ship across an ocean is the one that lives in the hand. It needs a reference, a delta record and an approval note.

Revision chaos

Twelve rounds of “one more tweak” without a record means the team cannot say why the fabric ended up where it did.

Performance late in the loop

A wicking or stretch requirement discovered in round eight re-prices the program. Performance belongs in the brief, not the revision.

02

What the service looks like for you

  1. 1

    Intent in, gaps named

    The tech pack, sketch or reference sample goes in. The sample plan comes back with the reference gaps listed — what we can sample against, what we need from you.

  2. 2

    Samples with comparisons

    Lab dips, strike-offs and functional trials arrive with written specification comparisons. The approval runs from the document, not the memory.

  3. 3

    Hand-feel as a record

    Where hand is the criterion, the reference defines it and the delta note records how the sample reads against it — approved, or revised with the reason.

  4. 4

    Revision history

    Every round adds to the record: what changed, against which reference, with which approval. The development history is part of the order file.

  5. 5

    Bulk handoff without re-baselining

    The approved sample hands into production on the same specification and acceptance criteria. No re-baselining, no drift at the handoff.

03

A development brief that works

ElementWhat to sendWhat we do with it
Design intentTech pack, sketch, reference garmentSets the construction shortlist
Hand-feel referencePhysical sample, or the closest availableDefines the hand criterion and the delta record
Target specComposition, weight, width — as knownSets the specification; gaps marked “need guidance”
Performance needsProperty, target, standard where knownSets the test plan before sampling, not after
Approver & criteriaWho decides, and on what basisSets the approval record format
TimelineThe decision date, not just the delivery dateSets the sample plan against it
04

Why the record matters at the season review

At the season review, the question is rarely “what did we make” — it is “why does it feel like this, cost like this, and behave like this?” A development team with the documented history answers in minutes: the reference, the deltas, the approvals, the test results, all in the order file. A team without it answers in a week of reconstruction, and usually wrong. The sampling workflow’s quiet product is that answer — the reason, recorded when it was known, instead of guessed when it is needed.
